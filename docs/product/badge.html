Badge & proof page

Show that you checked. Show exactly when.

An opt-in badge for your README or website. It shows the result of your app’s last PatchLabs check and the date it ran, and links to a public page anyone can read. It never claims more than that check proved.

What it says

Four states, each with a precise meaning.

The badge reads your app’s latest finished check. The date is always on it, so an old check looks old.

No known leaks

The last finished check found no personal data readable by an anonymous visitor or a signed-in stranger, on the tables we test.

Fix in progress

The last check found a leak. The badge doesn’t hide it: it stays amber until a re-check comes back clean.

Not checked yet

The app is verified and public, but no check has finished yet.

Private

The badge is switched off for this app, or the app isn’t verified. Nothing about the app is shown.

What the badge does not mean. “No known leaks” is the result of one dated check of who can read your database tables. It is not a certification, a guarantee, or a statement that the whole app is secure.

Proof page

Where the badge leads.

Clicking the badge opens a public page for the app. It confirms the repo, the current state and when it was last checked, and nothing an attacker could use.

Shows

  • The repo name
  • The current state, as on the badge
  • The date of the last finished check
  • What a PatchLabs check covers, and what it doesn’t

Never shows

  • Which tables leak, while a leak is open
  • Rows, columns or any data
  • The fix, or your report
  • Who is on your team

Questions

Badge FAQ.

Does the badge cost anything?

No. Badges are on every plan, including Free.

What happens if a new leak appears?

After the next finished check, the badge turns amber (“fix in progress”). It never pretends a leak isn’t there. The public page doesn’t name the table while the leak is open.

How fresh is it?

The image is cached for up to an hour. It always reads the latest finished check and shows that check’s date.

Can anyone make a badge for my repo?

No. Only a verified app with the badge switched on shows a result. Verification means committing a one-time code to the repo, which only someone with write access can do. Everything else shows “private”.

Can I verify the result myself?

Each finished check has an Ed25519-signed receipt. Your team can share a check link, and anyone can verify its receipt against our published key. How.
