PatchLabs vs Lovable security scan

Lovable checks your app as you build. PatchLabs proves the fix before it ships.

Lovable’s built-in security tools scan your app when you publish and can fix some issues inside the editor. PatchLabs is the independent check: it works on the code in GitHub, rebuilds your database, writes a migration and proves it as three different people before your engineer approves it. Use both: the builder’s scan while you build, an independent check before real users arrive.

Last checked 10 Nov 2026Sources at the endIndependent of Lovable

Choose Lovable’s scan when

You build and publish in Lovable and want checks with no setup at all.

  • You want a quick scan on every publish, in seconds, without leaving the editor
  • You want broad coverage: dependencies, and a deep scan of all app code for auth, payments, injection and leaked secrets
  • You want the Lovable agent to apply fixes inside your build
  • You want to block publishing when a critical issue is open

Choose PatchLabs when

You want each data-access fix proven, and reviewed by your engineer.

  • You want every fix tested as an anonymous visitor, a signed-in stranger and the owner before it ships
  • Your code is in GitHub, whether it came from Lovable, Bolt, Replit or Cursor
  • You want a migration file your engineer can read and approve, not a change made inside the editor
  • You want an independent second opinion with monitoring, alerts and signed receipts

The difference

A fix should be tested as the people it’s meant to stop.

An access rule that looks right can still let a signed-in stranger read other people’s rows, or lock the owner out of their own. We rebuild your database, apply the fix and run the same read as each person. Only when all three pass do we call it proven.

In our lab study, the pipeline fixed 20 new apps on its own: 18 of 20 fixes passed this proof with no human, and the other 2 went to a person (n=20, 9 Nov 2026).

supabase/migrations/…_patchlabs_leak_fix.sql
-- From our demo app. Proven: anon DENIED,
-- stranger DENIED, owner reads own row.
DROP POLICY "Profiles are viewable by everyone" ON public.profiles;
CREATE POLICY "Users can view own profile" ON public.profiles
  FOR SELECT USING (auth.uid() = user_id);

Questions

Asked by Lovable builders.

More on our page for Lovable apps.

Lovable’s scan passed. Do I still need this?

Not always. If your app holds no personal data, the built-in scan may be enough. If it stores emails, phone numbers, addresses or dates of birth, a proof that strangers can’t read them is worth having. In our study of 318 rebuilt apps, 106 let the wrong people read personal data (PatchLabs lab study, Nov 2026).

Will your fix conflict with changes Lovable makes?

The fix is an ordinary migration in supabase/migrations. Your engineer applies it like any other change, and our scheduled re-checks will tell you if a later change reopens the leak.

Is PatchLabs a Lovable connector?

No. We have no partnership with Lovable and nothing runs inside the editor. We work from your GitHub repo.

Do you check dependencies like Lovable does?

No. We focus on who can read your data, plus exposed keys in your code and git history. Keep Lovable’s dependency audit on.
