PatchLabs vs Keybrum

Keybrum attacks your live app. PatchLabs proves the fix before anything ships.

Keybrum (launched on Hacker News on 22 Nov 2026) runs hundreds of attacks against your deployed Lovable, Bolt, Replit, v0 or Cursor app, shows proof, and gives your coding agent a fix prompt, then attacks again. PatchLabs rebuilds your database from your migrations in a disposable lab, writes the fix as a migration, proves it as an anonymous visitor, a signed-in stranger and the owner, and only then hands it to the engineer who approves it.

You want your live app attacked from the outside, today, and you can pay now.

  • You want one run that covers Supabase, Firebase, API routes, storage, leaked keys and framework CVEs on the deployed app
  • You want a fix prompt to paste into Lovable, Bolt, Cursor or Claude Code, and a retest when your agent is done
  • You want a one-off paid report (€99) before a launch or a client handover, or a branded trust page for clients
  • You run many apps: their Team plan lists 25 apps for €199 a month, against 10 apps for £99 on our planned Agency plan

Choose PatchLabs when

Nothing should touch your live app, and a person must approve every change.

  • You’d rather no attack ran against your production app, its users’ rows or its keys
  • You want the fix proven before you see it, not checked after your agent has already changed production
  • You want a reviewable SQL migration your engineer (or your client’s) approves, with a signed receipt of the proof
  • You want a free check now: PatchLabs is free for the first 1,000 users in early access

Where we’re different

An agent-applied fix can break the owner’s own access. We check that before it ships.

A retest that fails for the attacker proves the stranger is locked out. Whether the owner can still read their own rows is a separate question, and on a live app the change is already in production when you ask it. Our lab runs the same read as all three people on the fixed copy, so a fix that locks out the owner, or only half closes the hole, is never called proven; it goes to a person.

  • No access to your database or cloud, and no attack on your live app
  • A person approves every change: yours, or your client’s engineer

Questions

Asked about Keybrum.

Can I use both?

Yes. Keybrum tests the running app from outside, across far more than the database. PatchLabs proves the database fix before it ships and re-checks it when migrations change. One looks at production, the other at the code that becomes production.

Is attacking a live app safe?

Keybrum says its attacks are built for live apps: writes are tested so no row is written, delete is never tested, and only counts, column names and one redacted row are kept. We haven’t verified that independently. We chose not to test live apps at all, so there’s nothing to trust on that point.

Keybrum is cheaper per app. Why pick PatchLabs?

On listed prices it is: 25 apps for €199 a month against our planned 10 apps for £99. If a live attack and a fix prompt are what you need, that’s a good deal. PatchLabs is for teams where nobody wants an agent changing production without a proven, approved migration, and it’s free during early access.

Are you affiliated with Keybrum?

No. We read their public pages on 22 Nov 2026 and did not run their paid attack. If anything here is wrong, tell us and we’ll fix it.
