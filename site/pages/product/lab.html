How it works

From migrations to a reproduced leak, in a lab we throw away.

Every Lab finding is something we made happen, not something we guessed from reading SQL.

  1. Rebuild

    Your supabase/migrations run in a disposable Postgres with Supabase’s roles, auth functions and default grants.

    migrations applied roles: anon, authenticated

  2. Seed

    Fake rows for two test users in every table, so there is something to read and something to protect.

    2 users · fake rows no real data

  3. Probe

    Query each table as an anonymous visitor (your public key), a signed-in stranger and the owner.

    anon → 2 LEAK stranger → 2 LEAK owner → 1 OK

  4. Report

    Each leak becomes a finding with an ID, the columns exposed, who can read them and the evidence.

    PL-001 · critical public.profiles

Why behaviour, not lint

A rule can look fine and still leak.

  • Read-only public tables show as intended, not as noise. If anyone may read your shop listings, mark them once and later checks remember.
  • Policies combine. Two harmless-looking policies can add up to “anyone can read”. The lab sees the result, not the parts.
  • Grants matter. We rebuild with Supabase’s default grants, so a table without RLS behaves exactly as it would in your project.
  • Owners must still work. Every probe includes the owner, so a “fix” that locks real users out is caught.

What the lab can’t see. It is only as complete as the migrations in your repo. Changes made by hand in the Supabase dashboard, or a backend other than Supabase/Postgres, are outside it, and the report says what couldn’t be rebuilt. It is not a pentest or a compliance certification.

Questions

Questions about this product line.

Something not covered? Email the founder.

Do you connect to my Supabase project?

No. We never need your database password or service key. The lab is a local Postgres rebuilt from the migrations in your public GitHub repo, seeded with fake rows and deleted after the run.

What if my migrations don’t build?

The report says which migration failed and what we couldn’t test. In our study, 318 of 436 repos rebuilt to a working database; the rest were mostly missing or out-of-order migrations.

How is this different from Supabase’s Security Advisor?

The advisor lints your live project’s settings, and it’s worth running. The lab tests behaviour: it signs in as each identity and counts the rows they get. Full comparison.

Are the five coming checks in my report?

No. PL-012 to PL-016 are announced so you know what’s next. They’ll appear in reports once they ship and are tested, and the changelog will say so.
